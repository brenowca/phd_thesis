\section{LTN Cross-layer Transcoder}
The methodology combines three components: sparse feature extraction, cross-layer alignment, and logic-based validation/steering. The design is grounded on two completed lines of work: (i) controlled late-generalization experiments with distribution shifts \cite{carvalho2025inducing_grokking_distribution_shifts}, and (ii) sparse-autoencoder analyses showing that small latent subsets can separate clean and perturbed samples in vision models \cite{carvalho2026interpretable_stego_detection_sae}. In this thesis, we transfer these insights to attention-based models under a unified pipeline.

Given a model and an input sequence, we collect residual activations $h_\ell$ at selected layers. We train sparse autoencoders (SAEs) to obtain sparse codes $s_\ell$, then learn cross-layer transcoders $T_{\ell\to k}$ to map features across depth. On top of $s_\ell$ and $\hat s_k$, we define soft logic validators (LTN-style predicates/rules) that produce differentiable satisfaction scores. These scores are used both for analysis (semantic consistency) and for control signals during inference (e.g., reranking, constrained decoding, and logit adjustments).

The key methodological claim is that local behavior is better diagnosed when feature-level geometry, cross-layer consistency, and rule satisfaction are measured together, rather than separately.

\section{Implementation Strategy}
\textbf{Stage 1: Reproducible data and model setup.}
We instantiate three families of experiments: (a) synthetic class/subclass datasets that induce measurable distribution shifts and grokking-like transitions; (b) language-generation tasks for steering; and (c) robustness tasks with adversarial/corruption perturbations. This follows the principle validated in previous work \cite{carvalho2025inducing_grokking_distribution_shifts,carvalho2026interpretable_stego_detection_sae}: stress-test the mechanism in controlled environments first, then transfer to more realistic benchmarks.

\textbf{Stage 2: Layerwise sparse feature learning.}
For each selected layer $\ell$, we train an SAE with reconstruction and sparsity objectives. We track reconstruction error, activation sparsity, dead-feature rate, and feature stability across seeds/epochs. This stage provides the canonical latent representation used downstream.

\textbf{Stage 3: Cross-layer alignment.}
We train $T_{\ell\to k}$ to predict layer-$k$ sparse codes from layer-$\ell$ sparse codes. Optimization combines alignment loss and regularization, with optional validator consistency penalties. We evaluate whether mappings preserve both numeric alignment and semantic behavior under validators.

\textbf{Stage 4: Logic-guided evaluation and steering.}
We define LTN-style predicates/rules on sparse features (and optionally outputs), producing satisfaction scores in $[0,1]$. These scores are used in two modes: evaluation-time diagnostics and inference-time steering. Ablations remove one component at a time (no SAE, no transcoder, no logic constraints) to isolate causal contribution.

\textbf{Stage 5: Statistical analysis and reproducibility.}
All reported results are aggregated over multiple seeds. We report confidence intervals for key metrics and publish scripts/configurations for exact reruns.

\section{Evaluation Tasks}
\textbf{Goal G1: Emergence and late generalization.}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Tasks: synthetic class/subclass benchmarks and grokking-prone setups from previous work.
    \item Behavioral metrics: train/test accuracy dynamics, delay to generalization, stability of post-transition performance.
    \item Mechanistic metrics: SAE sparsity/reconstruction, cross-seed feature stability, cross-layer alignment quality.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Goal G2: Steering locality and trade-offs.}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Tasks: constrained generation with single-rule and multi-rule settings.
    \item Behavioral metrics: constraint adherence, task quality, regression on non-targeted behaviors.
    \item Mechanistic metrics: validator satisfaction, layerwise activation shifts, consistency of targeted vs collateral feature changes.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Goal G3: Shift/attack diagnostics.}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Tasks: adversarial/corruption perturbation suites and controlled watermark-like perturbations.
    \item Behavioral metrics: detection ROC-AUC/PR-AUC, calibration, early-warning lead time.
    \item Mechanistic metrics: proportion of attack-sensitive sparse features, separability of clean vs perturbed latent codes, cross-layer signature persistence.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Baselines and ablations.}
Baselines include output-only heuristics (logit margin and confidence), dense-activation detectors, and steering without logic constraints. Core ablations remove each pipeline module and vary sparsity targets to quantify robustness of conclusions.

\section{Reproducible experimental settings}
This section consolidates concrete settings extracted from prior work so the methodology can be replicated with minimal ambiguity.

\textbf{G1 (late generalization under shift), grounded in prior work \cite{carvalho2025inducing_grokking_distribution_shifts}.}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Data design:} synthetic equidistant/equivariant class--subclass datasets with controlled sampling factor $f$ to induce train--test distribution shift; test set size fixed at 10,000.
    \item \textbf{Architectures:} Multi-layer Perceptron (primary report) and 2-layer Transformer (confirmatory trend).
    \item \textbf{Optimization defaults:} learning rate $10^{-3}$, weight decay $10^{-4}$, initialization scale factor $8$.
    \item \textbf{Statistical protocol:} aggregate key curves/metrics over 10 independent runs.
    \item \textbf{Compute reference:} single NVIDIA V100 (16GB) per run; batches of experiments under 24h in the reported setup.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{G3 (shift/attack diagnostics), grounded in prior work \cite{carvalho2026interpretable_stego_detection_sae}.}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Backbones/datasets:} pretrained ViT-B/16 (primary) with ViT-L/16 as a scale check;
    CIFAR-10 (ImageNet-10 retained as an optional secondary validation).
    \item \textbf{Input protocol:} upsample to $224\times224$ and normalize with ImageNet statistics; create clean/adversarial/stego variants from training splits and evaluate on held-out validation/test splits.
    \item \textbf{Attack settings:} FGSM with an $L_\infty$ budget swept over $\epsilon\in\{0.001,\dots,0.032\}$ (canonical $\epsilon=0.012$); PGD with $\epsilon=0.05$, 20 iterations, random initialization in the $L_\infty$ ball; plus low-opacity text-overlay steganographic perturbations.
    \item \textbf{SAE settings:} MLP-output activations at blocks $\{0,3,6,9,10,11\}$, mean-pooled over the 197 tokens; input dimension 768, overcomplete dictionary of 1024 latents; objective $\|h-\hat h\|_2^2+\lambda\|z\|_1$ with $L_1$ penalty $\lambda=0.1$; Adam, learning rate $10^{-3}$, batch size 256, 10 epochs; trained on clean activations only.
    \item \textbf{Detector protocol:} logistic-regression (and, as a non-linearity check, small-MLP) detectors on latent codes, evaluated under grouped $k$-fold cross-validation keyed to the source image; report ROC-AUC and PR-AUC.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{G2 (steering locality), protocol freeze and minimum evidence criteria.}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Frozen protocol window:} the definitive steering protocol is frozen after the February 2026 calibration cycle and before final March--April 2026 runs.
    \item \textbf{Frozen components:} predicate datasets, split policy, LoRA rank/alpha grid, decoder settings (including beam size/search policy), and the ablation matrix (no LTN, no $T_{\ell\to k}$, no SAE-guided features).
    \item \textbf{Minimum acceptance evidence:} report multi-seed gains in constraint satisfaction with bounded quality degradation and bounded collateral regressions against all steering baselines.
    \item \textbf{Failure policy:} if acceptance criteria are not met, G2 claims are downgraded to negative evidence and retained as explicit limitations rather than positive conclusions.
\end{itemize}

\section{G2 steering protocol (preliminary evidence track)}
\label{sec:g2-steering-protocol}
This section specifies the frozen G2 protocol in reproducible terms, using LSNC evidence as preliminary feasibility input (not as finalized quantitative proof). Predicate grounding uses a shared encoder with parameter-efficient LoRA adapters \cite{hu2022lora,sanh2019distilbert}. Each adapter predicts one predicate truth value (e.g., tense, voice, lexical constraint, or location-subject indicator), and predicate scores are composed into an LTN satisfaction score with differentiable logic operators \cite{badreddine2022ltn}.

For a partial sequence $Y_t=(y_1,\dots,y_t)$, candidate ranking uses a composite score:
\[
S(Y_t)=\log P_{\mathrm{LM}}(Y_t) + w_{\mathrm{logic}}\cdot \mathrm{Sat}_{\mathrm{LTN}}(Y_t),
\]
where $\log P_{\mathrm{LM}}$ captures language fluency and $\mathrm{Sat}_{\mathrm{LTN}}\in[0,1]$ captures rule satisfaction. We use guided beam search with the following loop per decoding step: (i) expand candidates with the base decoder, (ii) compute predicate truth values and $\mathrm{Sat}_{\mathrm{LTN}}$ for each hypothesis, (iii) rerank by $S(Y_t)$, and (iv) prune to the beam budget.

The evaluation suite covers two task families. \textbf{DFA-compatible controls} test lexical/ordering and simple structural constraints, allowing comparison with automata-based control baselines such as Ctrl-G \cite{zhang2024ctrlg}. \textbf{Syntax-sensitive controls} require sentence-level predicates (e.g., passive voice + past tense + location-based subject), where neural-symbolic composition is expected to be more expressive than regular-language control \cite{giunchiglia2024ccnplus}.

\section{G2 limitations and failure modes}
\label{sec:g2-limitations}
LSNC evidence is currently treated as preliminary because three limitations remain active until final frozen runs: (i) \textbf{latency scaling}, where inference cost grows with beam size, predicate count, and reranking frequency; (ii) \textbf{predicate error propagation}, where grounding errors can be amplified by logical composition; and (iii) \textbf{conflicting/impossible constraints}, where the controller may trade fluency for unsatisfiable targets. Final G2 claims are accepted only if these failure modes are bounded by the frozen acceptance criteria in this chapter.

\section{G3 SAE-based detection protocol (April 2026 freeze)}
\label{sec:g3-protocol}
This section specifies the frozen G3 protocol for SAE-based adversarial detection in vision transformers. The protocol was finalized on 2026-04-21 after a methodological refactor; the canonical execution documents (\texttt{PLAN.md}, \texttt{DESIGN\_CHOICES.md}, \texttt{RELATED\_WORK.md}) live alongside the codebase \cite{carvalho2026interpretable_stego_detection_sae} and are kept in lockstep with the experimental plan declared here.

\subsection{Pre-registered hypotheses}
The detector is evaluated against two adversarial hypotheses, each with numeric acceptance criteria declared before any sweep is executed.

\textbf{H1 --- Magnitude invariance.} A detector trained on SAE latents from clean and FGSM-attacked activations is hypothesized to achieve an AUC approximately constant across an FGSM $\epsilon$ sweep concentrated in the small-perturbation regime. Acceptance criteria, evaluated on $\epsilon \in \{0.001, 0.002, 0.004, 0.008, 0.012\}$: $|\mathrm{Pearson}\,r(\epsilon, \mathrm{AUC})| < 0.3$ \textbf{and} $\mathrm{var}(\mathrm{AUC}) < 0.001$ \textbf{and} $\mathrm{mean}(\mathrm{AUC}) > 0.95$. Larger $\epsilon \in \{0.1, 0.2, 0.3\}$ are kept only as ceiling sanity checks; a high AUC at $\epsilon=0.3$ proves nothing about H1 because the image itself is visibly destroyed.

\textbf{H2 --- Adversarial specificity.} A \emph{single} detector trained on \{clean, fgsm\} latents, evaluated on \{clean, benign\_$*$\} latents (gaussian noise, blur, JPEG compression, random dropout), is hypothesized to exhibit a separation gap. Acceptance criteria: $\mathrm{AUC}_{\mathrm{fgsm}} - \max_b \mathrm{AUC}_{\mathrm{benign}_b} > 0.3$ \textbf{and} $\max_b \mathrm{AUC}_{\mathrm{benign}_b} < 0.7$. Critical condition: the benign perturbation parameters are calibrated to match the FGSM mean L2 perturbation per image within $\pm 20\%$ at $\epsilon = 0.012$. Without L2 matching, gaussian noise at $\sigma = 0.1$ has roughly an order of magnitude greater pixel-space perturbation than FGSM at $\epsilon = 0.012$, which inverts the test.

Both criteria are pre-registered: thresholds are not adjusted after observing sweep results. A negative result for either hypothesis is reported as such and does not trigger threshold relaxation.

\subsection{Phase plan and exit tests}
The protocol unfolds in four phases. Each phase has objective exit tests (exit-code-0 commands or asserted Python checks); a phase is considered complete only when all of its tests pass and their raw outputs are recorded.

\textbf{Phase 1 --- SAE leakage isolation.} The sparse autoencoder is trained \emph{exclusively} on clean activations; its encoder is then frozen and used to produce latents for clean, adversarial, and benign inputs. The detector is logistic-regression on top. Health metrics are reported in \texttt{sae\_health.json}: reconstruction MSE (compared against an identity baseline), $L_0$ activation count (sparsity), and dead-feature fraction. A CLI flag \texttt{--train-on \{clean\_only, all\}} with default \texttt{clean\_only} makes the training population explicit and verifiable from logs.

\textbf{Phase 2 --- H1 sweep.} FGSM attacks are generated at the dense small-$\epsilon$ grid above. The sweep CSV reports both detector AUC and \emph{attack success rate} (ASR) per $\epsilon$; without ASR, AUC is not interpretable, since high AUC at near-zero ASR means the ``attack'' is not an attack. The decision rule for H1 is computed in a notebook that prints one of \texttt{GENUINE ADVERSARIAL DETECTION}, \texttt{MIXED SIGNAL}, or \texttt{TRIVIAL MAGNITUDE DETECTION}.

\textbf{Phase 3 --- H2 cross-validation.} A new CLI (\texttt{cross\_eval\_detector}) trains one detector on \{clean, fgsm\} latents and evaluates it on each \{clean, benign\_$*$\} pair. Magnitude calibration is automated: a separate report (\texttt{perturbation\_l2\_report.json}) records the mean L2 per image of each perturbation type and is checked against the $\pm 20\%$ window before evaluation runs.

\textbf{Phase 4 --- Feature analysis.} Conditional on H1 and H2 passing. Top-$k$ SAE features are ranked by detector weight times mean activation, and their per-population activation rates (clean / fgsm / benign) are reported. A visual gallery of max-activating patches and a layer-wise AUC sweep determine whether the discriminative signal is concentrated in interpretable mid/late features (consistent with semantic ``something is wrong'' processing) or spread uniformly across layers (consistent with low-level pixel-space signal).

A pre-execution Phase~0 (pipeline unblocking, methodology-neutral) was completed on 2026-04-21: a bug in which the ViT classification head was reinitialized randomly at load time --- causing FGSM gradients to be computed against a random head, invalidating any H1/H2 result derived from that pipeline --- was fixed via a CIFAR-10 linear-probe head trained over the frozen IN-1k backbone. This is recorded as design decision D1 below.

\subsection{Key design decisions}
Seven decisions are pre-registered in \texttt{DESIGN\_CHOICES.md} with motivation, alternatives considered, trade-offs, and revision triggers. The decisions load-bearing for G3 are summarized here.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{D1 --- Linear probe as base classifier.} A frozen ViT-B/16 IN-1k backbone with a linear head (\texttt{nn.Linear(768,10)}) trained on CIFAR-10 to $\geq 90\%$ validation accuracy. Adopted to ensure FGSM gradients flow against meaningful semantic features, not a random head.
    \item \textbf{D2 --- SAE trained on clean activations only.} Eliminates leakage by construction: the detector cannot exploit ``opportunistic'' SAE dimensions allocated to attacks seen in training. Trade-off accepted: SAE may reconstruct adversarial activations poorly, which is itself reported as a signal.
    \item \textbf{D3 --- Small-$\epsilon$ FGSM grid.} $\epsilon \in \{0.001, 0.002, 0.004, 0.008, 0.012, 0.016, 0.024, 0.032\}$ as the H1 core, with $\{0.1, 0.2, 0.3\}$ as ceiling sanity. The non-trivial regime for H1 is precisely small $\epsilon$, where attack success rate varies and the image remains perceptually indistinguishable.
    \item \textbf{D4 --- Single-detector cross-validation for H2.} One detector, multiple test distributions. Avoids the confound of comparing AUCs across detectors that each trained on different binary tasks (which tests solvability per task, not specificity).
    \item \textbf{D5 --- L2-matched benign perturbations.} Gaussian noise $\sigma$, blur kernel and intensity, JPEG quality, and dropout $p$ are all calibrated by binary search to produce mean L2 per image within $\pm 20\%$ of FGSM at $\epsilon = 0.012$. Without this matching, the H2 test is inverted by magnitude.
    \item \textbf{D6 --- Pre-registered decision criteria.} Numeric thresholds for H1 and H2 are declared in \texttt{PLAN.md} and in the analysis notebooks before the sweep is run. Thresholds may be relaxed \emph{before} a sweep with documented justification but never \emph{after} observing sweep results from the current campaign.
    \item \textbf{D7 --- CIFAR-10 as primary dataset.} Already cached locally; extensive adversarial-detection literature for direct baseline comparison; ten-class structure facilitates per-feature interpretability inspection. ImageNet-10 retained as an optional secondary validation.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Publication gate}
G3 claims are accepted for publication only if all of the following hold simultaneously: (i) Phase~0 sanity check passes; (ii) Phase~1 SAE health metrics within thresholds (reconstruction MSE below half of identity baseline, $L_0 \ll$ latent dimension, dead-feature fraction $< 30\%$); (iii) Phase~2 mean AUC $> 0.95$ in the small-$\epsilon$ regime with $|\mathrm{Pearson}\,r| < 0.3$; (iv) Phase~3 max-benign AUC $< 0.7$ and gap $> 0.3$ for all four benign perturbation types; (v) Phase~4 top-$k$ features show distinctly higher activation in FGSM than in matched benign. Failure of any gate downgrades the corresponding claim to a documented limitation, not an undisclosed weakness; partial passes are reported with the remaining claims explicitly weakened.

\subsection{Executed outcome}
The protocol was executed, and the pre-registered gates \emph{fail} by design:
detection AUC rises monotonically with the FGSM budget (H1 fails all three invariance
criteria) and matched-magnitude benign perturbations are nearly as detectable as the
attack (the H2 specificity gap is $\approx0.06$, far below the $0.3$ threshold).
Per the gate policy above, the corresponding claims are reported in their downgraded,
scoped form: the detector is characterized as a displacement-magnitude detector
rather than an interpretable-feature one, and the SAE is not load-bearing for the
detection. The Phase~4 feature analysis, gated on H1/H2 passing, therefore does not
run as a positive-result step; the layer-wise analysis instead corroborates the
low-level, magnitude-driven reading. The full results, tables, and their scoping ---
including the two flanks left to a planned re-run --- are reported in
Chapter~\ref{chpt:adv-detection}.

\subsection{Out of current scope}
Three extensions are deferred: (a) alternative SAE architectures beyond the linear-$L_1$ default and the sparsity-matched TopK check are tried only if the default fails Phase~1 health checks; (b) scaling to CLIP and vision--language models is deferred (ViT-L/16 is already included as a scale check in Chapter~\ref{chpt:adv-detection}); (c) adaptive attacks against the SAE-based detector and inter-model attack transfer are explicit non-goals of the current campaign, in line with the project's declared posture --- recorded in \texttt{DESIGN\_CHOICES.md} and \texttt{RELATED\_WORK.md} --- against framing the detector as a defense.

\section{Observed Effects}
The completed work supports the methodology in a more nuanced way than a
naive ``sparse features detect attacks'' reading would suggest, and the nuance is
itself a load-bearing finding:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Distribution shifts can reliably induce late generalization in controlled
    datasets, supporting the G1 experimental design.
    \item Sparse latent representations in vision models separate clean from
    perturbed inputs, but the completed characterization study
    (Chapter~\ref{chpt:adv-detection}) shows this separation is driven by
    off-manifold \emph{displacement magnitude} rather than adversarial-specific
    semantic structure: a high-$L_2$ attack (FGSM) is separated at AUC $1.0$ at
    \emph{every} layer including the first, while a near-zero-$L_2$ attack
    (steganography) is undetectable throughout.
    \item Critically for the G3 design, the SAE is \emph{not load-bearing} for this
    detection --- the sparse latents never exceed the raw activation, so
    interpretable features carry no detection signal that the raw activation lacks.
    That a compact detector matches a dense-activation baseline therefore
    corroborates, rather than contradicts, this reading: both read the same
    low-level displacement.
\end{itemize}

These observations sharpen, rather than merely motivate, the final thesis
experiments. The G3 study establishes what an activation-space detector actually
measures and where its instrument-level limits lie; the remaining work extends
sparse-feature diagnostics from this vision setting to attention-based steering and
cross-layer logical validation in a single framework, carrying the same discipline
of pre-registered gates and matched controls.
